# Lab-Grown Diamond Campaign Review Reference

## Main Failure Logic

Lab-grown diamond campaigns fail when they mix luxury emotion, natural-diamond symbolism, sustainability, and discount messaging into one incoherent promise. Force a single positioning choice before approving the brief.

## Reject / Revise Patterns

| ID | Situation | Judgment | Evidence basis |
|---|---|---|---|
| L1 | Uses natural-diamond "forever" romance without resolving lab-grown price deflation | Revise. The promise of permanence conflicts with known price decline and fashion-category perception. | De Beers/Lightbox repositioning and natural vs lab-grown category split. |
| L2 | Says "same as natural" as the core message | Revise. Chemical equivalence is not the same as symbolic, grading, resale, or luxury equivalence. | GIA 2025 policy shift away from applying traditional 4Cs grading to lab-grown diamonds. |
| L3 | Runs luxury, sustainability, and 30-70% cheaper messages together | Reject until positioning is singular. Mixed messages weaken both luxury and value. | Consumer surveys show price and sustainability are not universal primary drivers. |
| L4 | Over-focuses on engagement rings | Revise unless the brand can compete directly with natural-diamond bridal codes. | Growth opportunity is often fashion/self-gift, while bridal is natural diamond's defended core. |
| L5 | Claims environmental superiority without lifecycle or sourcing evidence | Reject until substantiated. | FTC greenwashing warning patterns and energy-intensity concerns. |
| L6 | Uses "MZ women" as a single target | Revise. Self-gift, bridal, and VIP/collector buyers need different channels and messages. | Korean department store and VIP purchase patterns differ from online self-gift behavior. |
| L7 | Ignores resale value | Revise. Silence can create negative WOM after purchase. | Lab-grown resale concern is a known luxury-buyer barrier. |
| L8 | Uses GIA 4Cs graphics or natural-diamond certification cues without clear lab-grown disclosure | Reject until compliance reviewed. | FTC Jewelry Guides require clear lab-grown disclosure; GIA policy context changed in 2025. |

## Yes/No Judgment Questions

1. Has the campaign chosen one positioning: fashion accessible luxury, bridal premium alternative, sustainable lifestyle, or technology innovation?
2. Does the brief define lab-grown as substitute, complement, or separate category?
3. Are price, store experience, channel, and message consistent with that positioning?
4. Is the target split into self-gift, bridal couple, or VIP/collector rather than one "MZ" group?
5. Are resale, environmental, and origin claims substantiated or handled transparently?
6. Is "lab-grown", "lab-created", or equivalent wording clear in consumer-facing copy?
7. Does the campaign avoid misleading natural-diamond grading or certification cues?
8. Is the brand-vs-activation balance appropriate for a high-consideration purchase?

## Persona Split

- P1 Fashion self-gift: 25-38, self-purchase, daily luxury, Instagram/TikTok/search.
- P2 Bridal couple: 28-35, joint decision, budget-driven larger-carat logic, Pinterest/store/YouTube education.
- P3 VIP/collector: older or high-net-worth, department-store/private consultation, gift or collection context.

Do not combine these into one campaign unless there are separate assets, channels, and claims for each.

## Good Message Shape

Single positioning, explicit lab-grown category label, persona-specific reason to buy, and verified origin/environment data when used. Avoid "same as natural + cheaper + sustainable + forever" bundles.
