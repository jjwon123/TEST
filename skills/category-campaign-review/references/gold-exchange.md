# Gold Exchange / Precious Metals Campaign Review Reference

## Main Failure Logic

Gold exchange and precious metals campaigns operate in a trust-asymmetric market. Review trust, fee transparency, tax clarity, and regulatory wording before creative quality. Financial fear and guaranteed-return messages are high-risk.

## Reject / Revise Patterns

| ID | Situation | Judgment | Evidence basis |
|---|---|---|---|
| G1 | Promises profit, guaranteed hedge, principal safety, or fast gains | Reject. This resembles deceptive investment or advisory claims. | FTC/CFTC/SEC precious metals enforcement patterns, including markup and profit-promise cases. |
| G2 | Uses impersonation, fake news styling, or financial authority mimicry | Reject. This is platform and regulator high-risk behavior. | Metals.com / TMTE-style microtargeted impersonation pattern. |
| G3 | Uses fear marketing such as dollar collapse, bank freeze, deep state, or retirement panic | Reject or legal review. Fear-based elderly targeting is a known enforcement pattern. | Safeguard Metals / Red Rock-style elderly investor enforcement cases. |
| G4 | Centers Instagram/TikTok for high-value older investors | Revise. Channel-persona mismatch and financial ad policy risk. | Gold IRA and precious metals buyer age/asset patterns. |
| G5 | Provides investment-advice style consultation without registration boundary | Reject until compliance review. | Korea capital markets and advisory regulation risk; US RIA/FINRA/SEC concerns. |
| G6 | Promotes semi-numismatic or limited coins without transparent spot value and markup | Reject until pricing basis is clear. | Red Rock-style controlled-market and excessive markup pattern. |
| G7 | Does not show trust signals such as KRX, LBMA, Korea Mint, WGC RGIP, BBB, or equivalent | Revise. In a lemons market, trust signals are the product. | WGC Retail Gold Investment Principles and market signaling logic. |
| G8 | Hides VAT, withdrawal tax, fees, spread, or markup | Reject until transparent. | KRX gold market VAT and withdrawal treatment; transparency principle. |
| G9 | Mixes investor, collector, and gift buyer messages | Revise. These are different CEPs with different risk tolerance and emotional logic. | Korean gold-bar, gift, coin, and KRX market structure. |

## Yes/No Judgment Questions

1. Is the target one of investor, asset-building entrant, gift buyer, or collector?
2. Does the copy avoid return, principal, safety, and crisis-profit promises?
3. Are spot price, markup, spread, fee, storage, resale, and tax/VAT conditions visible?
4. Are trust signals visible above the fold or early in the script?
5. Is the channel matched to the persona's age, trust behavior, and buying cycle?
6. Does the campaign avoid unregistered investment-advice language?
7. Does it avoid fear-based retirement or macro-collapse claims?
8. Does the brief explain custody, authenticity, sourcing, and redemption?

## Persona Split

- P1 Asset-holder investor: 50-70s, diversification/inflation CEP, newspaper, TV, PB, KRX, store consultation.
- P2 Asset-building entrant: 30-45, small purchases, search, YouTube education, KRX app.
- P3 Gift buyer: broad age, baby gift, wedding, promotion, retirement, Naver/store/department store.
- P4 Collector: coins and limited editions, specialist communities and expert media.

P1 and P3 should not share one main message. Investment rationality and gift emotion pull in opposite directions.

## Good Message Shape

Price transparency, authentication, custody/redemption options, tax clarity, trust signal visibility, and education. Avoid profit promises, macro panic, anonymous testimonials, and vague "safe asset" shortcuts.
