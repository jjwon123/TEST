# Skincare Campaign Review Reference

## Main Failure Logic

Skincare failures usually come from claim substantiation gaps, vague targets, channel-message mismatch, and trust-damaging proof choices. Review every brief as a substantiation problem first and a creative problem second.

## Reject / Revise Patterns

| ID | Situation | Judgment | Evidence basis |
|---|---|---|---|
| S1 | Target is "MZ women", "2030 women", or another broad demographic | Revise. This does not define a skin concern, buying trigger, price tier, or information channel. | Korean MZ is a local marketing aggregate; Millennial and Gen Z differ in income, media use, and buying triggers. |
| S2 | Ingredient list is the main idea, especially "clean", "free-from", or competitor ingredient fear | Revise or reject if fear-based. Ingredient marketing can become chemophobia and does not prove outcome. | FTC "all natural" enforcement patterns; dermatology criticism of overbroad clean beauty claims. |
| S3 | Claims imply drug-level effects such as gene activation, DNA repair, treatment, 7-day younger skin, clinically proven without product-level proof | Reject until substantiation exists. | FTC L'Oreal Genifique/Youth Code case; NAD skincare claim reviews. |
| S4 | Influencer/UGC plan lacks clear sponsorship disclosure | Reject until disclosure rules are explicit. | FTC Endorsement Guides; NAD influencer disclosure decisions. |
| S5 | AI/CGI skin, filtered before-after, or unrealistic model proof carries the campaign | Revise. It conflicts with the category's need for real skin proof. | Beauty trust backlash and "creative humanity" industry critique. |
| S6 | Teen/tween anti-aging or adult-study claims used for children | Reject until legal and substantiation review clears it. | CARU/FTC child endorsement scrutiny; adult consumer-use tests cannot prove child outcomes. |
| S7 | Same creative is planned for Instagram and TikTok | Revise. Instagram is stronger for brand aesthetics; TikTok/search behavior demands proof and "does it work" content. | Channel behavior benchmarks and influencer platform guidance. |
| S8 | Category trust crisis is ignored, such as SPF underperformance, clean beauty backlash, or AI backlash | Revise. A category trust shock lowers campaign credibility if not addressed. | Purito SPF trust crisis as category-risk pattern. |

## Yes/No Judgment Questions

1. Is the target defined by a concrete CEP rather than age/generation only?
2. Does the brief name the skin concern, expected outcome, price tier, and primary discovery channel?
3. Does every efficacy claim have product-specific substantiation?
4. Are drug-claim trigger words avoided or legally reviewed?
5. Are influencer material connections disclosed clearly in the creative plan?
6. Does the channel plan separate Instagram aesthetics from TikTok proof/search content?
7. Is the message one core benefit plus proof, rather than an ingredient dump?
8. Does the brief state how to handle category trust risks?

## Target Definition Standard

Require all four:

- CEP / buying trigger, such as pre-wedding glow, post-breakout calming, morning five-minute routine
- concrete skin concern and outcome expectation
- price tier: prestige, masstige, or mass
- first information channel: TikTok, Instagram, YouTube, Olive Young, Naver, dermatologist, etc.

## Good Message Shape

One benefit, credible proof, sensory use experience, and distinctive brand asset. Do not approve "MZ loves it", ingredient overload, medicalized anti-aging promises, or filtered before-after proof.
